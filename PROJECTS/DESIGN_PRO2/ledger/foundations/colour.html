Paper, ink, and one green

The palette is small on purpose. Warm paper is easier on the eyes over a long day than bright white. Black ink carries the primary action. Green appears only where something is selected or in focus.

Surfaces

Three levels: the page, things that sit on it, and wells sunk into it. Depth comes from these tones first. Shadows are only for things that float.

Ink for action, green for state

Most systems use their brand colour for the primary button. We don't. A black button stands out on warm paper without shouting. That leaves green free to mean "this is selected" or "you're here", and that meaning stays consistent everywhere.

Dark theme

Dark mode is warm too, more like a desk lamp than a terminal. Every token keeps its name and gets a value designed for the dark surface; nothing is inverted automatically. Green becomes lighter so it still reads, and the primary button becomes paper-coloured.
