Charts

Four chart types cover nearly everything a finance team asks. Each one is drawn in plain SVG, answers to hover, and can be read as a table.

Script assets/charts.jsMarkup <figure data-chart>

Start with the question

Choose the chart from what the reader needs to find out. Sometimes the answer is a single number in a stat tile, with no chart at all.

The reader asksUse
How do these compare?Bars. Vertical for time periods, horizontal for long category names.
How has it moved?A line. Two lines at most if you're comparing, and both on the same scale.
What's it made of?A 100% stacked bar. Not a pie: people can't compare angles accurately.
How did we get from A to B?A waterfall. Profit bridges, budget-to-actual, cash movements.
What's the number?A statistic. Don't draw a chart for one value.

Bar chart

Bars sit flat on the baseline with rounded tops. A 2px gap separates bars in the same group. The y-axis always starts at zero, because bar length is what the reader compares.

Line chart

2px lines, with markers only on hover. If there are four lines or fewer, each one is labelled at its end so readers don't have to check the legend. Mark notable events on the chart itself.

100% stacked bar

For parts of a whole, compared across groups. Keep it to six segments at most, and merge anything smaller into "Other". Segments are separated by a 2px gap and labelled where they're wide enough.

Waterfall

Shows how one total becomes another. Totals are drawn in ink, increases in green and decreases in clay. Every bar has its value printed on it, so colour is never the only cue.

Series colours

Six hues, always assigned in the same order and never repeated. Each colour stays with its entity: if a filter removes a series, the others keep their colours. Any series past the sixth goes into "Other". The order was checked by script so that neighbouring colours stay distinct for people with red-green colour blindness, in both themes.

Never

  • Two y-axes. Readers will compare the lines anyway, and the comparison means nothing. Draw two charts instead.
  • A number on every point. Label the first value, the last, and anything worth commenting on. Hover shows the rest.
  • Status colours for series. Red and green mean good and bad. A series called "Glasgow" is neither.
  • Coloured text. Labels and values stay in ink. The coloured mark beside them shows which series they belong to.
  • 3D, gradients, shadows on marks. They make values harder to read.
