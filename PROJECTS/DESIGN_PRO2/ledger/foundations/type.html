A serif to read, a sans to work

Newsreader sets headings and big numbers: it has the warmth of a printed report. Instrument Sans sets everything you interact with, because its figures are clear at 13px. IBM Plex Mono is for codes, references and tokens.

Three families

Display & numbers Aa 0123 Newsreader Page titles, section heads, statistics, dialog titles. Never used for UI labels.
Interface Aa 0123 Instrument Sans Body, labels, buttons, tables. Tabular figures are switched on wherever numbers line up.
Codes Aa 0123 IBM Plex Mono Invoice numbers, account codes, IBANs, eyebrows. Codes that people copy or read aloud.

Scale

Nine sizes. The small end goes up in 1px steps because a dense table needs that precision. The large end grows by about a third at each step.

Numbers

Our users read numbers more than words, so numbers get the most care. Use tabular figures in columns so the digits line up. Use a proper minus sign. Show currency once, in the header.

House rules

  • Sentence case everywhere. "Post journal", not "Post Journal". Title Case looks shouty in a dense UI.
  • Line length 50–75 characters for anything people read as prose. Set max-width in ch.
  • Two weights in the sans: 400 and 600, with 500 for buttons and labels. Never use bold inside a sentence for decoration.
  • The serif never goes below 16px. Below that its details turn to mush on Windows.
  • Codes in mono when people will copy them or read them over the phone. "INV-2041" in mono is hard to misread.
